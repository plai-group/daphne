Write a stochastic simulator in Clojure:

(let [;; Sample hidden risk profiles
      user-risk (sample (beta 2 8))        ;; ~20% baseline
      merchant-risk (sample (beta 1 20))   ;; ~5% baseline
      device-risk (sample (beta 1 50))     ;; ~2% baseline
      
      ;; Transaction data: [amount, typing-speed, vpn-score]
      transactions [[50.0   0.95  0.15]    ;; Normal: small, human-like
                    [80.0   1.05  0.20]    ;; Normal
                    [120.0  1.10  0.18]    ;; Normal
                    [500.0  2.6   0.80]    ;; SUSPICIOUS: large, fast, VPN
                    [750.0  2.8   0.85]]   ;; FRAUD: very large, bot, VPN
      
      ;; Process all transactions (more on next slide)
      result (reduce process-transaction 
                     [user-risk merchant-risk device-risk 0] 
                     transactions)
      
      fraud-count (nth result 3)]
  
  [user-risk merchant-risk device-risk fraud-count])
#+END_SRC

** The Transaction Processor
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:transaction-processor
:END:

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
(defn process-transaction [risk-acc txn-data]
  (let [user-risk (first risk-acc)
        merchant-risk (second risk-acc)
        device-risk (nth risk-acc 2)
        fraud-count (nth risk-acc 3)
        
        ;; Extract transaction features
        amount (first txn-data)
        observed-typing (second txn-data)
        observed-vpn (nth txn-data 2)
        
        ;; Compute fraud probability from risk factors
        amount-effect (* 0.01 (- amount 100.0))
        base-fraud-prob (+ (* 0.4 user-risk) 
                          (* 0.4 merchant-risk) 
                          (* 0.2 device-risk) 
                          amount-effect 
                          -0.4)
        fraud-prob (sigmoid (* 5.0 base-fraud-prob))
        
        ;; Sample: is this transaction fraudulent?
        is-fraud (sample (bernoulli fraud-prob))
#+END_SRC

** Transaction Processor (continued)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:transaction-processor-2
:END:

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
        ;; Behavioral signals depend on whether it's fraud
        typing-speed (if is-fraud 
                        (sample (normal 2.5 0.3))  ;; Fast (automated)
                        (sample (normal 1.0 0.3))) ;; Normal human
        
        vpn-score (if is-fraud
                     (sample (beta 8 2))   ;; High VPN usage (hiding)
                     (sample (beta 2 8)))] ;; Normal connection
    
    ;; Observe the behavioral signals (with noise)
    (observe (normal typing-speed 0.2) observed-typing)
    (observe (beta 10 10) observed-vpn)
    
    ;; Return accumulator with updated fraud count
    [user-risk merchant-risk device-risk 
     (+ fraud-count (if is-fraud 1 0))]))
#+END_SRC

*** Graphical Probabilistic Programming Language (GPPL)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:gppl
:END:

*Key insight*: First-order programs (no recursion) have a fixed number of random variables

- All random variables can be identified *without executing* the program
- Graph structure can be extracted at *compile time*
- Based on FOPPL (van de Meent et al. 2018), extended with higher-order functions

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
;; This is parametric - we know the structure
(let [x (sample (normal 0 1))
      y (sample (normal x 1))]
  (observe (normal y 1) 2.0)
  [x y])

;; This is NOT - structure depends on random value
(loop [i (sample (poisson 10))]  ;; Unknown number of iterations!
  ...)
#+END_SRC

*** Multi-pass Compilation Pipeline
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:compilation-pipeline
:END:

#+ATTR_HTML: :width 1200px
[[./assets/daphne_pipeline_placeholder.svg]]

1. *Desugar*: Transform Clojure syntax to core forms

*** Partial Evaluation: Key Technique
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:partial-evaluation
:END:

Partial evaluation allows =reduce= over data while maintaining parametric structure:

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
;; Input program
(reduce observe-data [slope bias] 
        [[1.0 2.1] [2.0 3.9] [3.0 5.3]])

;; After partial evaluation (unrolled)
(let [acc1 (observe-data [slope bias] [1.0 2.1])]
  (let [acc2 (observe-data acc1 [2.0 3.9])]
    (observe-data acc2 [3.0 5.3])))
#+END_SRC

- Data values known at compile time
- Loop structure can be unrolled
- Results in fixed graph with all =observe= statements visible

** Structured Continuous Normalizing Flow (SCNF)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:scnf
:END:

*** Continuous Normalizing Flow
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:cnf-basics
:END:

CNF is an expressive variational family for amortized inference:

- Transforms base distribution (Gaussian) to complex posterior via ODE
- Provides both sampling and probability evaluation
- Parallel computation (no sequential dependencies)

#+BEGIN_LATEX
\begin{align}
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\,\color{red}{\vx}(t) &= f_{\theta}(\vx(t),t,\color{blue}{\vy}) \\
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\,\ln q(\color{red}{\vx}(t), t) &= -\nabla_{\vx} \cdot f_{\theta}(\vx(t),t,\color{blue}{\vy})
\end{align}
#+END_LATEX

(Chen et al. 2018, Grathwohl et al. 2019)

*** Structured CNF: Use Graph Structure
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:scnf-structure
:END:

#+ATTR_HTML: :width 80%
[[./assets/compilation_x_notation.svg]]

- Use graph structure to build sparse neural network
- Faithful inversion for initialization
- *Problem*: Unstable in high dimensions, expensive training (2 ODE solves per gradient step)

(*Weilbach et al. 2020*)

** Graphically Structured Diffusion Model (GSDM)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:gsdm
:END:

*** Why Diffusion Models?
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:why-diffusion
:END:

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) overcome CNF limitations:

- *Stable* in high dimensions
- *Much cheaper* to train (simulation-free, one time step per gradient)
- *State-of-the-art* density estimation (Ho et al. 2020)
- Can be *trans-dimensional* (Campbell et al. 2023)

#+ATTR_HTML: :width 1000px
[[./assets/denoising_process.png]]

(Ho et al. 2020)

*** Denoising Objective
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:denoising-objective
:END:

DDPMs reduce to a simple L2 loss:

#+BEGIN_LATEX
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}_{p(\color{red}{\vx_0}, \vx_t, \obs)} \left[ \lambda(t) \cdot \lVert \hat{\vx}_\theta(\vx_t, \obs, t) - \color{red}{\vx_0} \rVert_2^2 \right]
\end{align}
#+END_LATEX

- Predict clean data $\color{red}{\vx_0}$ from noisy observation $\vx_t$
- Conditioned on observations $\obs$
- Flexible embeddings for $\lat$, $\obs$, $\marg$ (Tashiro et al. 2021, Harvey et al. 2022)

*** Sparse Transformer Architecture
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:sparse-transformer
:END:

For $n$ graphical model nodes with query, key, value embeddings $\mQ,\mK,\mV \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$:

#+BEGIN_LATEX
\begin{align}
  \emb^\text{out} &= \emb^\text{in} +\softmax\left( \color{red}{\mM} \odot \mQ \mK^{T} \right) \mV
\end{align}
#+END_LATEX

- $\color{red}{\mM}$: Masking matrix derived from graph adjacency
- Only allow attention along graph edges (sparse)
- Project graph structure into neural network architecture
- Permutation invariances → positional embeddings

*** GSDM Translation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:gsdm-translation
:END:

#+ATTR_HTML: :width 1400px
[[./assets/gsdm-fig1.png]]

- Use *forward graphical model* structure (as if everything is latent)
- Extract *graph dependencies* → attention mask
- Link functions are *optional* (can be learned)
- *Flexible conditioning*: any subset of variables can be latent/observed/marginalized

(*Weilbach et al. 2023*)

*** Why This Works
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:why-gsdm-works
:END:

*Inductive bias*: Attention mask matches the causal structure

- Variables only depend on their graph parents
- Reduces $O(n^2)$ attention to $O(e)$ where $e$ = number of edges
- For Monad Lisa: 30 edges vs 38² = 1,444 possible connections
- Enables scaling to larger problems

Learned attention patterns (unconstrained) vs imposed graph mask (sparse)

** Step 3: Topological Ordering
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:topological-ordering
:END:

*Problem*: Variables must be generated in dependency order

#+BEGIN_SRC python
# BAD: Using variables before they're defined
observe19_val = Normal(sample15_val if sample14_val ...).sample()
sample14_val = Bernoulli(...).sample()  # Defined AFTER use!
#+END_SRC

*Solution*: Topological sort of dependency graph

#+BEGIN_SRC python
# GOOD: Dependencies first
sample14_val = Bernoulli(...).sample()
sample15_val = Normal(2.5, 0.3).sample()
observe19_val = Normal(sample15_val if sample14_val ...).sample()
#+END_SRC

Critical fix for programs using =reduce= with observations in data structures!

(Implementation: =src/daphne/gsdm.clj=, uses =daphne.core/topo-sort=)

    def run_simulator(self):

        sample4_val = Beta(2, 8).sample()    # user-risk

        sample6_val = Beta(1, 50).sample()   # device-risk

            sample8_val if sample7_val else sample9_val,

    def get_graphical_model_mask_and_indices(self):
        indices = [set([i]) for i in range(38)]
        
        # Add edges from graph structure
        indices[4].add(5)   # user-risk -> is-fraud-1
        indices[5].add(4)   # (bidirectional for attention)
        indices[4].add(12)  # user-risk -> is-fraud-2
        indices[5].add(12)
        indices[7].add(3)   # is-fraud-1 -> observe-12
        ...
        
        # Convert to mask tensor
        max_attendable = max([len(idx_set) for idx_set in indices])
        mask = torch.zeros(38, max_attendable, dtype=torch.long)
        for [i, idx_list] in enumerate(indices):
            mask[i][slice(None, len(idx_list))] = 1
        
        return [mask, indices_tensor]
#+END_SRC

** Discussion: Design Trade-offs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:discussion-design
:END:

*Current Limitation*: Daphne works best as a command-line compiler

Why not a Clojure library with macros?

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
;; Imagined API (doesn't work well currently)
(defmodel fraud-detection
  [user-risk (sample (beta 2 8))
   ...]
  ...)
#+END_SRC

*Trade-off*:
- ✅ CLI: Clean separation, works with any editor/REPL
- ✅ CLI: Easy to integrate into build pipelines
- ❌ CLI: Less "Clojure-like", separate compilation step
- ❌ Macros: Harder to extract graph from macro expansion
- ❌ Macros: Compilation happens at Clojure compile-time, not runtime

*** Lambda Latte: Coffee Shop Pricing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:lambda-latte
:END:

Similar structure to Monad Lisa:

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
(defn process-customer [acc data]
  (let [base-rate (second acc)
        price (nth acc 2)
        price-sensitivity (nth acc 3)
        
        purchase-rate (* base-rate 
                        (sigmoid (* (- price-sensitivity) 
                                   (- price 8.0))))
        buys-latte (sample (bernoulli purchase-rate))]
    
    (observe (bernoulli (if buys-latte 0.9 0.1)) data)
    
    [(+ total-revenue (if buys-latte price 0.0))
     base-rate price price-sensitivity]))
#+END_SRC

*** Bayesian Linear Regression
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:blr
:END:

Classic example with =reduce=:

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
(defn observe-data [acc data]
  (let [slope (first acc)
        bias  (second acc)
        xn    (first data)
        yn    (second data)
        zn    (+ (* slope xn) bias)]
    (observe (normal zn 1.0) yn)
    [slope bias]))

(let [slope (sample (normal 0.0 10.0))
      bias  (sample (normal 0.0 10.0))
      data  [[1.0 2.1] [2.0 3.9] [3.0 5.3] 
             [4.0 7.7] [5.0 10.2] [6.0 12.9]]]
  (reduce observe-data [slope bias] data)
  [slope bias])
#+END_SRC

*** Partial Evaluation Implementation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h:partial-eval-impl
:END:

Key functions in =src/daphne/partial_evaluation.clj=:

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
(defmulti partial-evaluation dispatch-partial-evaluation)

(defmethod partial-evaluation 'reduce [exp]
  (let [f (second exp)
        init (third exp)
        coll (fourth exp)]
    (if (and (vector? coll) (every? can-eval? coll))
      ;; Unroll the reduce
      (reduce-at-compile-time f init coll)
      ;; Keep as is
      exp)))
#+END_SRC

Fixed-point operator ensures all nested expressions are evaluated.

#+BEGIN_SRC clojure
(defn program->graph [program]
  (let [desugared (desugar program)
        partially-evaluated (partial-eval desugared)
        graph (extract-graph partially-evaluated)]
    {:V (vertices graph)
     :A (adjacency graph)
     :P (procedures graph)
     :Y (observations graph)}))
#+END_SRC

Topological sort in =daphne.core/topo-sort= ensures correct ordering.
